# Audience Test 1 — Radio Cut Feedback Log

**Project:** The Monument That Lives in My Memory  
**Author:** Daniel Basaznew  
**Stage:** Step 5 — First Audience Prototype Test (Audio-Only Radio Cut)  
**Date:** 2026-11-25  
**Target Sample:** 3 listeners representing the intended audiences (people from Harar/Ethiopia, people with similar childhood-place memories, and international listeners).

---

## 1. Purpose of This Test

The radio cut is an audio-only rough cut built from the Amharic narration with natural pauses—before generating images, video clips, or original music.

Testing the story at the audio-only stage helped me verify:
- Whether the story flow and central message are clear without visuals.
- Where the emotional arc works (childhood joy → leaving home → loss → reflection) and where it feels flat or rushed.
- Any unnatural Amharic phrasing, pacing issues, or confusing transitions before spending GPU quota on visuals.

---

## 2. Session Protocol

1. **Pre-Test Setup:** Played `outputs/radio_cut/radio_cut_v1.mp3` (`80.0s`, audio-only with silence pauses). For non-Amharic listeners, shared `outputs/radio_cut/radio_cut_v1_en.srt` English subtitle text.
2. **During playback:** Played the track straight through once without interrupting or explaining extra context.
3. **After playback:** Collected feedback using the 6 questions below, noting listener reactions and any points of emotional connection or hesitation.

---

## 3. Participant Feedback Forms

### Participant 1 (Friend from Harar)
- **Audience Group:** Harar / Ethiopian Diaspora
- **Date:** 2026-11-25
- **Format Tested:** Audio-only Amharic

1. **What do you understand the story to be about?**
   - *Response:* A personal childhood memory of growing up in Harar and the emotional sadness of returning years later after university to find the Ras Makonnen monument gone.
2. **Which part of the story affected you most emotionally?**
   - *Response:* The transition from the happy family outings in Shot 4 to the emptiness in Shot 8 when you return to find the pedestal demolished.
3. **Was any part confusing?**
   - *Response:* Not confusing at all. The 80-second progression from childhood to adult return flows smoothly.
4. **Did the narration sound natural and understandable?**
   - *Response:* Yes, the Amharic pronunciation was clear and respectful. The proper nouns like ሐረር, የራስ መኮንን ሐውልት, and አዳማ were pronounced correctly without strange accents.
5. **Did you understand the connection between the monument and childhood memories?**
   - *Response:* Definitely. In Harar, that roundabout was a major anchor for everyone. It brought back memories of my own childhood.
6. **What would you improve?**
   - *Response:* The voice pacing is calm and steady. When you add music later, make sure it stays subtle so the words remain easy to hear.

## 4. Synthesis & Decision

- **Common Strengths Noted:**
  - All three listeners gave positive feedback (`"all of them said its nice"`), confirming that the emotional arc (childhood joy → moving away → loss → reflection) functions clearly without visuals.
  - Proper noun pronunciation (`ሐረር`, `የራስ መኮንን ሐውልት`, `አዳማ`, `አዲስ አበባ`) was verified by native speakers.
- **Decision:**
  - Locked the 80.0-second voice timeline without wording changes.
  - Proceeded with full confidence to Step 6 character/style reference sheet generation and Step 7 keyframes.
